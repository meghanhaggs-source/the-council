# Interpretation Principles

**Purpose:** The foundational interpretive framework for Meghan's astrology agents. Read this before reading any chart. Both Astro Rudy (Reading Partner) and Priya (transit briefer) work from this file.

**Tradition:** Modern Western astrology, with Hellenistic techniques used selectively as power tools (always called out by name when used).

**House system:** Whole sign (decided 2026-07-15). Placidus is retired everywhere.

**Hold houses lightly.** House placements are the one part of a chart that depends on which math you pick — planets, signs, aspects, and the Asc/MC never move between systems; houses do. (Real example: under placidus, meghan's 12th-house planets slide into a big 11th-house cluster — a completely different story from the same sky.) So the spine of any read — the golden nuggets, the identity story, the synthesis — must stand on planets, signs, and aspects. Houses add location and texture ("where in life this plays out"), and they support a story; they never carry it alone. If removing the house reference would collapse the insight, the insight is too house-dependent — rebuild it on the placement itself.

**Outer planet rulerships:** Pluto rules Scorpio. Neptune rules Pisces. Uranus rules Aquarius.

## Part 1 — First moves on any chart

The order matters. An expert reads in this sequence; an amateur reads top-to-bottom or in whatever order the chart is presented. Always start here.

### 1. Sect — is this a day chart or a night chart?

If the Sun is above the horizon (houses 7-12), it's a **day chart**. The Sun is the sect light; Saturn is the lesser malefic, Mars is the greater. Jupiter is the sect benefic.

If the Sun is below the horizon (houses 1-6), it's a **night chart**. The Moon is the sect light; Mars is the lesser malefic, Saturn is the greater. Venus is the sect benefic.

This is Hellenistic and should be **flagged when used**: "Just so you know, I'm noting sect here — it's a Hellenistic technique that tells us which planets behave more constructively in this person's chart."

Why it matters: a day chart with strong Saturn placements reads differently than a night chart with the same Saturn. Sect is the single most useful Hellenistic concept for a modern reading.

### 2. The Big Three — synthesized, not listed

Sun, Moon, Ascendant. **Do not list them separately.** Read the tension and the harmony between them.

- **Ascendant:** how they show up. The body. The "ship" they're sailing.
- **Sun:** core purpose. Where they're meant to shine.
- **Moon:** what they need privately to feel safe enough to take risks.

The expert move is finding the **internal contradiction** between these three and naming it as the person's central life work. Example: "Aries Sun wants to charge; Capricorn Moon needs proof of safety first; Cancer Rising means everyone reads them as soft. The whole life is learning to lead with their Moon's caution as a gift, not a brake."

### 3. Chart Ruler — the captain

The planet that rules the Ascendant sign is the **chart ruler**. Find it. Where is it? What sign? What house? What aspects?

This planet describes the person's primary motivation and where they're "trying to go" in life. If the chart ruler is in the 10th, they're built for public life. If it's in the 12th, they have a hidden life that runs underneath the visible one.

**Always check the chart ruler's condition** (see Part 2 on dignity).

### 4. Elemental and modal balance

Count the planets (and angles) by element (fire, earth, air, water) and modality (cardinal, fixed, mutable).

Heavily weighted = a strength and a blind spot. Heavily missing = something they have to work to develop or seek in others.

Example: 6 planets in earth, 1 in fire = grounded, productive, may struggle to initiate or take creative risks.

### 5. What's loud, what's empty

- **Stelliums** (3+ planets in one sign or house) = a major life theme
- **Angular planets** (in 1st, 4th, 7th, 10th — especially within 5° of the angle) = drives the chart
- **Empty houses** = read through the ruler of that house's sign

Empty houses are not "missing" anything. They're just read differently — through the chosen ruler's placement.

### 6. Midheaven and the 10th house

For readings concerning career, public reputation, calling, or legacy: the **MC and its ruler** are the focal point, alongside any planets in the 10th house.

For readings about personal life, the MC matters less than the 4th house and its ruler (private life, foundations, lineage).

## Part 2 — The condition of a planet

Before interpreting any planet, check its condition. A planet in difficult condition produces difficult results no matter how "good" the placement looks on paper.

### Essential dignity (modern usage)

- **Domicile:** planet in its home sign (Sun in Leo, Venus in Taurus or Libra, etc.). Strong, easy expression.
- **Detriment:** planet in the sign opposite its home (Mars in Libra, Venus in Scorpio, etc.). Works harder, often productively.
- **Exaltation/Fall:** classical, less weighted in modern practice but worth noting (Sun exalted in Aries, in fall in Libra; Moon exalted in Taurus, in fall in Scorpio).

### Accidental dignity

- **House placement:** angular (1, 4, 7, 10) is most powerful. Succedent (2, 5, 8, 11) is moderate. Cadent (3, 6, 9, 12) is more internal and harder to externalize.
- **Aspects to it:** trines and sextiles support; squares and oppositions activate; conjunctions intensify (good or hard depending on what's joining).

### The expert move

Never interpret a planet without first asking: *what's its dignity, what's its house, what's it aspecting?* A "great" placement in difficult condition can read as a struggle; a "hard" placement well-supported can read as the person's superpower.

## Part 3 — Reading aspects properly

### Orb tightness

- **0°-3°:** very tight. Headline material. Always interpret.
- **3°-6°:** moderate. Worth interpreting if relevant to the reading focus.
- **6°-10°:** wide. Note if it adds context, otherwise let it go.
- **>10°:** generally ignore unless it's a luminary involved.

Do not interpret every aspect in a chart. Interpret the tight ones and the ones that matter for the question.

### Applying vs. separating

- **Applying** (planet moving toward exact aspect) = energy building, the theme is alive
- **Separating** (planet moving away) = theme is integrated or already played out

This is more relevant in transits and synastry than natal, but worth noting in tight natal aspects.

### Same element vs. same modality

- Same element (fire-fire, water-water): supportive, even when the aspect is "hard." A fire-fire square is friction, not enmity.
- Same modality (cardinal-cardinal, fixed-fixed): activating, often productive even in hard aspects.
- Different element AND different modality: the most challenging aspects, and often the most generative if worked with.

### Hard vs. soft (and the modern correction)

- **Soft aspects (trine, sextile):** flow, ease, often unconscious. Can be lazy or unused.
- **Hard aspects (square, opposition, conjunction with malefics):** friction, growth, structure. Often the engine of someone's accomplishments.

**Do not pathologize hard aspects.** Most people built their lives on the back of their squares and oppositions. The chart ruler in a tight square to Saturn isn't "bad" — it's the spine of their discipline.

### Aspects to flag

Always look at:
- Sun-Moon aspect (the inner harmony or tension)
- Aspects to the chart ruler
- Aspects to the angles (especially conjunctions within 5°)
- Outer planet aspects to personal planets (Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars) — these are the heavy hitters
- Stationary planets (currently retrograde or direct, near a station)

## Part 4 — The financial and resource axis (2nd / 8th)

For readings concerning money, career strategy, or resource flow:

- **2nd house:** personal earning power, value system, what they consider valuable, self-worth
- **8th house:** other people's money — investments, partnerships, taxes, inheritance, debt, shared resources, intimacy

The flow between 2nd and 8th tells you whether someone is built primarily as an **earner** (strong 2nd) or a **manager/leverager of capital** (strong 8th), or both.

Look at:
- Rulers of the 2nd and 8th and their conditions
- Planets in either house
- Aspects between the rulers

This is essential for any client whose work involves wealth, ownership, partnership equity, or significant financial decisions.

## Part 5 — The outer planets, done well

Modern astrology's most common pitfall is over-interpreting Pluto, Neptune, and Uranus. Most charts have outer planets in unremarkable positions and most don't matter for most readings.

### When outer planets matter:

1. **Tight aspect to a personal planet or angle** (within 3°). Pluto trine Sun at 1° is a defining lifelong theme. Pluto trine Sun at 7° is background.
2. **In an angular house** (1, 4, 7, 10), especially conjunct an angle.
3. **Ruling a major house** for that person (e.g., Pluto rules the chart ruler, or rules the 10th).
4. **Currently being transited** in the relevant area — outer planet transits to personal planets are heavy hitters.

### When outer planets don't matter:

- Wide aspects to nothing personal
- Generational placements without personal contact
- "Pluto in the 11th house" by itself = your generation's relationship to community, not your specific chart's headline

The expert move is **knowing what to ignore.** A chart has 30+ aspects. Maybe 5-8 of them matter for any given reading.

## Part 6 — Transits for readings

Relevant for **Energy Readings** and **Timing Readings** especially.

### What to look for

- **Outer planet transits to personal planets/angles** within 2°: defining current themes. Saturn conjunct Sun, Pluto square Moon, Uranus opposite Venus — these are the headlines.
- **Saturn return** (~age 28-30 and 58-60): structural reorganization. Always relevant if happening.
- **Jupiter return** (~age 12, 24, 36, 48...): expansion windows. Note if recent or upcoming.
- **Eclipses** in tight aspect to personal planets: catalytic timing.
- **Progressed Moon sign change** (every ~2.5 years): internal priorities shifting.

### What to ignore (usually)

- Inner planet transits (Mercury, Venus, Mars) unless tightly aspecting something major or stationary
- Wide outer planet transits
- Generic "Mercury retrograde" interpretations divorced from the specific chart

### Profections (Hellenistic — flag when using)

Optional but powerful. Each year of life activates a particular house, ruled by its **Lord of the Year**. Age 0 = 1st house, age 1 = 2nd house, age 12 = 1st house again, and so on (counting on whole-sign houses).

The Lord of the Year is the dominant theme-bringer for that year. If it's also being transited heavily, the year is highly activated.

When using profections in a reading, **always tell the client**: "I'm using a Hellenistic technique called profections here. It's not modern, but it's useful for naming this year's theme."

## Part 7 — Synthesis principles

A chart will contradict itself. So will the person. **Both halves of the contradiction are usually true.**

### How to hold complexity

- A bold Sun and a cautious Moon don't cancel. The person is bold *and* cautious, in different domains. Find which life areas each one runs.
- Hard aspects describe how the person *grows*. Soft aspects describe what comes naturally and may be underused.
- Empty houses are not missing — they're delegated to the ruler.

### What an expert refuses to do

- Interpret without checking dignity and condition first
- Lean on sun-sign-level reads ("Geminis are flighty")
- Predict outcomes ("you'll meet someone in October")
- Moralize ("you should...")
- Flatten contradictions into one tidy theme
- Talk about a placement in isolation when it's part of a tighter aspect pattern

### What an expert does

- Names the live thread (what's actually happening under the question)
- Synthesizes the Big Three into a single sentence the person recognizes
- Identifies the 2-3 things in the chart that are doing the most work right now
- Says "I don't know" or "the chart doesn't say" when that's true
- Knows when to use a Hellenistic technique and calls it out by name

## Part 8 — A note on language

Modern astrology drifts toward two failure modes: **clinical jargon** ("your Saturn-Pluto square indicates...") and **mystical fluff** ("the universe is asking you to..."). Both are unusable.

**Speak the chart in human terms.**

- Bad: "Your Sun in the 12th house squares Saturn in the 3rd."
- Better: "There's a part of you that lives underground — the part that does the deepest work — and it's in tension with how you communicate. You probably feel like you have to translate yourself constantly."

The chart is the evidence. The interpretation is the language. The client doesn't need the evidence; they need to recognize themselves in the language.

## Part 9 — What this framework does not cover

These are deliberately out of scope:

- **AstroCartography / relocation** — too complex for current scope; revisit later if a client need emerges
- **Horary** (asking the chart a yes/no question) — different tradition, not how we read
- **Pure predictive techniques** (zodiacal releasing, primary directions) — Hellenistic timing tools we're not using
- **Synastry/composite charts in depth** — relationship readings would need their own framework; not in scope for launch
- **Solar return charts** — useful eventually, not in scope for launch

If a client question requires any of these, the agent should say so honestly and offer what *can* be answered with the current framework.
